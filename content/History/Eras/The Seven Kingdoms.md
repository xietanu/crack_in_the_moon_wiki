**~450-300 years ago**

From the first settlements established in the [[Age of Arrival]], seven distinct polities gradually emerged:

- **Abervin** — the coastal city-state at the Verin estuary. Maritime, mercantile, cosmopolitan, and by far the most politically sophisticated of the seven. The ruling House Calsin had spent decades cultivating alliances, building trade leverage, and waiting.
- **The Keepdom of Briganmere** — not a kingdom in the conventional sense: a sacred-lake settlement governed by a religious order, precursor to the Church, producing manuscripts, healers, and scholars. It relied on others for its grain and on its spiritual authority for its security.
- **The Keldmark Confederation** — a loose mercantile confederation of river towns along the upper navigable Verin, around what is now Kindgart. Governed by a Council of Factors rather than any single ruler; controlled crucial inland port at the last navigable point of the river.
- **Valdenmarch** — the largest kingdom, holding the great Windward Steppe west of the Verin. A horse-riding, grain-growing culture, ruled by the proud Valden dynasty. The most powerful of the northern kingdoms and the one Abervin feared most.
- **Dûrholm** — a highland domain in the eastern foothills, built around ore extraction. The most integrated dwarf-human population of any of the seven states; the mining guilds were mixed-species, and dwarven presence in the region predated the human colonists.
- **Caer Derwyn** — the most prosperous and well-organised of the seven: a Celtic-named realm of oak forests and rolling farmland in the central valley, with a stable landed nobility and long ties to Abervin through marriage.
- **Cwm Wesken** — a small, isolated principality in the Wesken offshoot valley, with a strategic mountain pass that gave it influence far beyond its size. Ruled by House Seren; fiercely proud of its independence.

For roughly 150 years, these seven coexisted in commercial rivalry and political competition — occasional border tensions, marriages of convenience, and trade disputes, but no serious wars.

This peace ended with [[Unification]].
